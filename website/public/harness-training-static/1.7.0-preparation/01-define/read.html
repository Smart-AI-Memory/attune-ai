Describe what the person needs

A good goal explains the useful result. It does not force you to specify the implementation.

A clear reporting goal

Turn the supplied activity CSV into a readable report so a person can explain total time by project and category without manually adding the rows.

That goal tells the assistant why the report matters. It also gives you a way to assess the result: can a reader find the totals and connect them to the input?

  1. Name the input: the supplied synthetic CSV.
  2. Name the reader's useful result: understand where the time went.
  3. Name what the output must show: entry count, total minutes, project totals and category totals.
  4. Ask what remains unclear before accepting the work.

Your assistant can recommend an approach and explain its tradeoffs. You settle choices that change the result, such as the intended reader or output format.

Choose your starting path: this bounded reporting exercise starts from a clear goal. If your real project has several workflows or unresolved requirements, use the goal-driven and spec-driven reference to choose the preparation it needs before accepting a plan.

Say how you will recognize success

Use concrete examples and a failure case. A polished appearance alone cannot establish that the totals are right.

  • Completeness: all six valid entries appear in the count, without a hidden filter.
  • Totals: the report shows 270 minutes; Orion 180 and Lumen 90.
  • Categories: Development 150, Documentation 75 and Testing 45.
  • Invalid input: an invalid row produces a helpful error and no misleading partial report.
  • Preservation: the input and protected checks remain unchanged.

Ask your assistant to connect each criterion to an independent check. Later you will inspect the actual results and separate review, including concerns the chosen checks may miss.

A saved draft records what you intend. Approval records your decision about the reviewed scope. Starting execution remains a separate action.

Which success criterion is useful before the reporting work starts?

The six supplied entries total 270 minutes, and project totals account for that same total. This is observable and can be checked independently from the source rows.

The report looks professional, so its totals can be assumed correct. Appearance can help the reader, but the totals still need independent checks against the input.
