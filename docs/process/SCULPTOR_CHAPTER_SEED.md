# The sculptor — chapter seed

---

The marble is the data. It holds more statues than you will ever
carve.

You and your agent are co-sculptors, holding different chisels.
You decide what the statue is. The agent carves fast and shows
every stroke. Its chisel adapts — edge, angle, pressure — to the
grain of whatever material you hand it, drawing on more carving
knowledge than any one sculptor could hold.

But the chisel never chooses the statue.

Most of the work is removal. The discipline isn't in what you
generate — anyone can pour clay. It's in what you refuse to keep,
and in knowing the difference between carving (deciding form —
yours) and casting (replicating a decided form across surfaces —
delegated, gladly).

One story, for the record. I once proposed a new feature to my
agent — a fifth member of a small family of interaction forms we'd
built together. The agent disagreed. Not by ignoring me, and not
by building it anyway: it raised a formal objection, with a
concrete alternative — the shape I wanted already existed as a
render variant of what we had, at a fifth of the code. I looked at
its case and switched. The feature was never written, and the
system is better for it.

That's the whole metaphor in one motion: the chisel felt the grain
argue against the cut, and it spoke instead of carving. The
statue stayed mine to choose. It got simpler because I wasn't
holding the only chisel — and because the other one knew its job
was advocacy, never the last word.
